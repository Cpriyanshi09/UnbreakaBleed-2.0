Breast Cancer Awareness

Knowledge is Your Fist Line of Defence

Learn about Breast Cancer symptoms, risk factors, and prevention strategies. Early detection saves lives.

What is Breast Cancer?

Cancer happens when the cancerous cells grows and multiplies and becomes tumors. Similarly, in breast cancer cancerous cells multiplies proliferately in breast tissues and forms tumors. Mostly women of age 50 or older are the most prone to be affected by breast cancer. Having said that, it can affect women lesser than age 50 as well. Breast cancer doesn't just happen in women as everyone is born with some breast tissue, thus anybody can be affected with breast cancer. Finding the cancer earlier makes it much more likely that the cancer can be cured.

Types of Breast Cancer

There are majorly 13 types of breast cancer. They are classified on the basis of:

1. Invasive Ductal Carcinoma (IDC)

->The most common type (70–80%).
->It starts in the milk ducts and multiplies in the surrounding breast tissue.
->It can spread to lymph nodes or other parts of the body as well.

2. Ductal Carcinoma in Situ (DCIS)

->It usually is non-invasive in nature, however, can be invasive if not treated.
->These are abnormal cells in the ducts but has not invaded in rest of the body/ outside body.

3. Invasive Lobular Carcinoma (ILC)

->It is second most invasive breast cancer in nature.
->It begins in the milk producing glands, also called lobules.
-> It may not be detected by mammogram as well.

4. Lobular Carcinoma in Situ (LCIS)

->It is not true type of cancer, but is an abnormal growth in the milk producing glands.
->It increases the future risk of developing breast cancer in both the breasts.
1. Inflammatory Breast Cancer (IBC)

->It is aggressive in nature,where cancer cells blocks the lymph vessels in the skin.
->It causes redness, swelling, warmth, orange peel-like texture (peau d’orange).
->It is inavsive in nature.

2. Paget’s Disease of the Breast

->It is a rare cancer which starts in milk ducts, and spreads to nipple/areola skin.
->It causes itching, redness, flaking skin, discharge around the nipple.
->It is often linked with DCIS or invasive cancer.

3. Angiosarcoma

->This type of cancer resides in the blood or lymph vessels of the breast.
->It may develop as a primary cancer or secondary (after radiation therapy).
->It is extremely aggressive.

4. Male Breast Cancer

->This type of cancer occurs in men (about 1% of all breast cancers).
->Most of them are invasive ductal carcinomas.
->These are often diagnosed late because men don’t expect breast cancer.

5. Recurrent Breast Cancer

->This typer of cancer that comes back after treatment.
->It can be Local (same breast or scar area), Regional (nearby lymph nodes) or Distant/Metastatic (spread to other organs).
1. Hormone Receptor–Positive (ER+/PR+)

->The cancer cells grow in response to estrogen (ER) or progesterone (PR).
->It can be treated with hormone (endocrine) therapy.

2. HER2-Positive Breast Cancer

->It has too many HER2 proteins on cancer cells, making them grow fast.
->The targeted therapies (like trastuzumab) are effective.

3. Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC)

->It lacks ER, PR, and HER2 receptors.
->It is pretty aggressive, limited treatment options (mainly chemotherapy, immunotherapy).

4. Metastatic Breast Cancer (Stage 4)

->This type of cancer can spread beyond the breast to bones, liver, lungs, or brain.
->It can be considered incurable but treatable for life extension and quality of life.

Understanding Risk Factors

While having risk factors doesn't mean you'll develop breast cancer, understanding them can help you make informed decisions about your health

    1. Lifestyle-related Risk Factor

  • Alcohol consumption
  • Obesity, especially after menopause
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking or tobacco use
  • Unhealthy diet (high in saturated fats, low in fiber)
  • Long-term night shift work (disruption of circadian rhythm)
  • Exposure to radiation (especially to chest area)

    2. Estrogen-related Risk Factors

  • Early menarche (before age 12)
  • Late menopause (after age 55)
  • Long-term use of hormone replacement therapy (HRT), especially combined estrogen-progestin therapy
  • Postmenopausal obesity (fat tissue produces estrogen)
  • High lifetime exposure to endogenous estrogen

    3. Reproductive Factors

  • Late age at first full-term pregnancy (after age 30)
  • Never having children
  • Not breastfeeding or short duration of breastfeeding
  • Use of certain fertility treatments (may increase hormone levels)
  • Shorter duration of pregnancy-free intervals

    4. Family History and Genetic Factors

  • Family history of breast cancer (especially in a first-degree relative: mother, sister, daughter)
  • Inherited gene mutations - BRCA1 and BRCA2
  • Personal history of breast cancer or other cancers

    5. Aging-Related Risk Factors

  • Increasing age (most cases occur after age 50)
  • Age-related genetic mutations in breast cells
  • Decline in immune system function with age
  • Cumulative exposure to risk factors over time
Remember: Having one or more risk factors doesn't mean you will get breast cancer. Many women with risk factors never develop the disease, while others with no known risk factors do.

Prevention and Early Detection

While not all breast cancers can be prevented, there are steps you can take to reduce your risk and catch any changes early.

Healthy Lifestyle

Maintain a healthy weight, exercise regularly and limit alcohol consumption.

Regular Screenings

Follow recommended mammography schedules and perform monthly self-examinations.

Know Your History

Understand your family history and discuss genetic testing if recommended
